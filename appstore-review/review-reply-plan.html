3 · What the app does, who it is for

Trunex is a private, invitation-only network of adults who vouch for one another, so that a member can find trustworthy help from a real person anywhere they happen to be.

The problem it solves. When something goes wrong away from home — a breakdown on a foreign motorway, an unfamiliar city, a job that needs doing by someone who will not take advantage — open directories and public marketplaces cannot tell you who is actually trustworthy. Anyone can list themselves, and no one is accountable for the listing.

How Trunex answers it. Every member is personally invited and vouched for by an existing member, and that chain of vouching is visible on each profile, so trust is traceable to a named person rather than to a star rating. Members publish the skills they have and the geographic zones they know well. Another member can then search by skill, by area, or along a planned route, message the person directly, and see exactly who vouched for them. If something goes seriously wrong, an SOS alerts the ten nearest members and opens a group conversation with them.

The value. A member travelling, working or living away from home is never dependent on strangers with no accountability: they reach someone a member they trust has already vouched for.

Audience. Adults, 18 and over. Drivers and long-distance travellers, tradespeople, small business owners, and people living or working abroad. The app is not directed at children and contains no content aimed at them.

4 · Setting up and reaching the main features

  1. Launch the app and allow location when asked. The map opens with members shown near you. Positions are deliberately offset by 200–300 m; Trunex never displays a member's exact location.
  2. Tap "Sign in" and use the demo credentials above.
  3. Finding people — on the map, use "By skills", "By zone" or "By route". Tap any pin to open a member profile and see the vouching chain.
  4. User-generated content — the Stories tab. Tap "Share a story" to post text and a photo.
  5. Reporting and blocking — open any member profile, tap the safety action at the top right: Block is immediate, Report opens a category picker. Blocked members are listed under You → Settings → Blocked members.
  6. Messaging — the Chats tab, one-to-one and group.
  7. Businesses — the Business tab lists services run by members and recommended by other members.
  8. SOS — the orange button on the map. Please do not complete an SOS: it alerts ten real members and opens a live conversation with them. The confirmation screen shows the full flow without sending.
  9. Account deletion — You → Settings → Delete account. It is immediate and irreversible; please use the second demo account for it.

No sample files are required.

5 · External services used

  • Cloudflare Workers and Workers KV — application backend, hosting and data storage.
  • Mapbox GL — maps and geocoding.
  • Apple Push Notification service, addressed through Firebase Cloud Messaging — push notifications.
  • Resend — transactional email (sign-up, invitations, notifications).
  • Twilio — SMS one-time codes during sign-up.
  • Cloudflare Turnstile — anti-abuse verification on sign-up.
  • Anthropic Claude — used server-side only, for automated screening of member-submitted text against our community guidelines, and for drafting community email copy. It is not exposed as a chat or content-generation feature to members, and member data is not used to train any model.
  • Stripe — used only by the trunex.app website. The iOS app contains no purchase flow of any kind, opens no payment page, and never reaches Stripe.

Paid content. Version 1.4.0 ships entirely free. There are no in-app purchases, no subscriptions and no paid features, and no StoreKit products are declared for this build. There is therefore no purchase flow to demonstrate.

6 · Regional differences

The app behaves identically in every region. There is no region-locked content, no regional pricing, no geographically restricted feature, and no regional variation in what a member can see or do. The interface is in English everywhere. The member network is worldwide, so the people shown on the map differ by location — that is the product working as intended, not a regional feature difference.

7 · Regulated industries and third-party material

Trunex does not operate in a regulated industry. It provides no medical, legal or financial advice, processes no payments in this version, and is not an emergency service. The SOS feature states plainly, in the app and before every send, that it is not an emergency service, is not monitored around the clock, and that the user should call their local emergency number first.

All content, branding and source code are owned by SC DKR Engineering SRL. Map data is licensed from Mapbox under their commercial terms. The app contains no third-party protected material.

Notes for the reviewer

  • Please do not send a live SOS — it alerts ten real members and opens a conversation with them.
  • Account deletion is real and immediate; the second demo account is provided for it.
  • The app requests location only while it is in the foreground. It has no background location capability and no tracking; it does not present the App Tracking Transparency prompt because it tracks nothing.

5 · Risks that could earn us a second rejection

These are things I found in the code that a reviewer may run into. Each needs a decision from you.

R1 — The demo account must be pre-approved on production. If we hand them an account that lands on "Your application is with us", we get rejected again on the same ground. The account has to be a full approved member, on production trunex.app (not staging), with two-factor off, and it must not be suspended or expire during review.
R2 — The Donate row is visible on iOS but does nothing. "Donate" appears under You for every platform, but with billing off the native rail is unavailable and the call to action is disabled with the message "In-app purchases are not available in this version yet". A reviewer who taps it finds a dead end, which is a fair 2.1 finding. Two ways out: hide the row on iOS in a 1.4.1 build, or leave it and pre-empt it in the notes. My recommendation is to hide it — it costs one conditional and removes the finding entirely.
R3 — Turnstile on sign-up. The reviewer may try to register before reading the notes. If the anti-abuse check misfires on their network, they will read it as a broken sign-up. Worth a line in the notes telling them to use the demo account rather than registering.
R4 — A reviewer may send a live SOS. It alerts the ten nearest real members. The written warning above helps, but it is a request, not a guard. Tell me if you want a code-level guard for flagged demo accounts; that is a change to the next build, not something to fix in the reply.
