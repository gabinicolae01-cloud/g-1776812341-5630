You do not upload it to Apple. That answers the size worry: nothing large ever goes near App Store Connect. You record it on the iPhone, put it on YouTube as Unlisted (not private, not public — unlisted means only someone with the link can open it), and paste that link into the reply. And it is not hundreds of gigabytes. An iPhone screen recording of four minutes is a few hundred megabytes at the very most — the same order as a few dozen photographs. It uploads to YouTube from the phone over normal wifi in a couple of minutes.

Four minutes is plenty. I said four to seven before; four is enough. It is not a film and nobody is judging it. A reviewer needs to watch a person use the app and see that it works.

How to record it

  1. On the iPhone: Settings → Control Centre → add Screen Recording (once, if it is not already there).
  2. Delete Trunex and install it again from TestFlight. This matters: iOS only shows a permission request the first time, and Apple asked to see those requests.
  3. Go to the iPhone home screen. Swipe down, tap the record button, wait for the countdown.
  4. Walk through the app as in §2. Take your time.
  5. Stop the recording. It saves to Photos. Share it to YouTube, set it to Unlisted, copy the link.

3 · What the app does, who it is for, and the problem it solves

Trunex is a private network of people who vouch for one another, so that anyone can reach someone they can actually trust, anywhere in the world, at the moment they need help.

It is not aimed at a particular kind of person. The premise is that everyone needs help with something at some point, in some field, in some place — and that a stranger from a search result is not the same thing as a person somebody you trust will answer for.

An example of a real request the network is built for: a member in Bucharest has a daughter who has just moved to Paris to work in a restaurant kitchen. She needs somewhere trustworthy to live. He opens Trunex, finds members in Paris whom other members have vouched for, and asks them to help her find a place. Note that neither the person asking nor the person being helped is a tourist, and the person who needs the help is not the one making the request. That is normal on Trunex, and it is the point.

The problem. When you need help in a place or a field you do not know, the open internet offers strangers with no accountability — anyone can list themselves, and nobody stands behind them. Reviews can be bought. A recommendation from a person who has to look their friend in the eye afterwards cannot.

How Trunex answers it. Every member is personally invited and vouched for by an existing member, and that chain of vouching is visible on every profile, so trust traces back to a named human being rather than to a rating. Members publish the skills they have and the areas they know, so another member can search by skill, by area, or along a route, and contact a real person directly. If something goes seriously wrong, an SOS alerts the ten nearest members and opens a group conversation with them.

Who it is for. Adults, 18 and over, anywhere in the world. It is not directed at children and holds no content aimed at them.

4 · Setting up and reaching the main features

  1. Launch the app and allow location. The map opens showing members near you. Positions are deliberately offset by 200–300 m — Trunex never shows anyone's exact location.
  2. Sign in with the reviewer credentials above.
  3. Finding people — on the map, search by skills, by zone, or along a route. Tap any member to see their profile and the chain of members who vouched for them.
  4. Member-created content — the Stories tab; "Share a story" posts text and a photo.
  5. Blocking and reporting — open any member's profile and use the safety action at the top right. Block takes effect immediately; Report opens a list of reasons. Blocked members are listed under You → Settings → Blocked members.
  6. Messaging — the Chats tab, one-to-one and group.
  7. Businesses — the Business tab lists services run by members and recommended by other members.
  8. SOS — the orange button on the map. Please open it to see the flow but do not send: it alerts ten real members and starts a live conversation with them.
  9. Deleting an account — You → Settings → Delete account. It is immediate and permanent.

No sample files are needed.

5 · Outside services the app depends on

  • Cloudflare Workers and Workers KV — the backend, hosting and data storage.
  • Mapbox — maps and place search.
  • Apple Push Notification service, addressed through Firebase Cloud Messaging — notifications.
  • Resend — email.
  • Twilio — the SMS code sent during registration.
  • Cloudflare Turnstile — anti-abuse check on registration.
  • Anthropic Claude — on our servers only, to screen member-submitted text against our community guidelines before a human moderator sees it. It is not a feature members interact with, and member data is not used to train any model. This is disclosed in our privacy policy.
  • Stripe — used only by our website. The iOS app has no purchase flow, opens no payment page, and never reaches Stripe.

Paid content: none. Version 1.4.0 is entirely free, with no in-app purchases, no subscriptions, and no StoreKit products declared.

6 · Regional differences

There are none. The app behaves identically everywhere, in English, with no regional pricing, no region-locked content and no feature that varies by country. Members are spread across the world, so the people shown on the map differ with your location — that is the product working, not a regional difference.

Trunex is not in a regulated industry. 4 · What I got wrong before, corrected here

The "reviewer hits a wall" claim was false. I wrote that a reviewer would land on "Your application is with us". The code says otherwise: /api/guest/config exists specifically so that, in the worker's own words, "store reviewers open an invitation-only app with no account; this is how they get in". A reviewer who opens the app browses as a guest. The waiting screen only appears to someone who completes a full registration. I inferred it from the sign-up path and stated it as fact.
The audience was wrong. I described Trunex as being for travellers, expatriates and people away from home. It is for anyone who needs help, and the person asking need not be the person who needs it. §3 above is rewritten around that.
Where that wrong idea came from, and why it matters beyond this document. The app's own Community Guidelines screen says "Trunex activates when you travel. It helps you find trusted people nearby — a human connection when you're far from home." That is in the shipped build, and it is what Apple reads too. Your product copy is describing something smaller than your product. Worth fixing in the next build — say the word and it is a five-minute change.
